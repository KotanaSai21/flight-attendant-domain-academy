Why this matters for our systems
Our software sits inside a chain of people who each own part of a flight. Understanding their roles tells
us who consumes our data, when they need it, and what breaks when it is late or wrong.
Shared vocabulary
Sequence, duty period, segment and base mean
specific things. Using them precisely prevents costly
requirement errors.
Time is operational
Crew data drives real-time decisions at the gate.
Latency and stale records have immediate
downstream cost.
Rules are non-negotiable
Contract and regulatory rules constrain what the
system may legally assign to a crew member.
Many consumers, one truth
Gate, ground, crew tracking and the FA all read the
same schedule. Consistency matters more than
features.

Ground staff

• Push back and marshal the aircraft

Crew: flight deck and cabin
“Crew” covers two distinct workgroups with separate contracts, separate bidding systems, and separate
qualification rules — but they fly the same sequence structure.
Flight deck
Captain · First Officer
• Qualified by aircraft fleet and seat
• Bid by seniority within base and fleet
• Governed by flight time and duty limits
Cabin crew
Purser · Flight Attendants
• Qualified by aircraft door and language
• Bid by seniority within base and position
• Governed by duty, rest and staffing
minimums
From here on, the deck focuses on cabin crew — the flight attendants our systems serve.

Two groups: lineholders and reserves
Each bid month, every flight attendant lands in exactly one of these groups. The distinction changes how the
system schedules, notifies and pays them.
Lineholder
Holds a published line of sequences
• Awarded a full month of sequences by
seniority bid
• Knows the schedule in advance; plans life
around it
• Can trade, drop and pick up trips within
contract rules
• System focus: bidding, trading and schedule
integrity
Reserve
On call to cover the operation
• Assigned availability windows rather than
fixed trips
• Called out to cover sick calls, delays and
misconnects
• Governed by call-out, rest and maximum-
days rules
• System focus: availability, notification and
legality
Seniority decides the split — junior flight attendants typically sit on reserve until seniority lets them hold a line.

The scheduling hierarchy
S E Q U E N C E
A group of duty periods flown as one trip. Starts and
ends at the same crew base. Typically one to four days.
D U T Y P E R I OD
One working day: report, fly, release.
Bounded by duty-time limits and followed
by a rest period.
F L I G H T S E G M E N T
A single leg from one airport to
the next. Also called a flight or a
“leg.”
A sequence contains duty periods; a duty period contains flight segments. Rest periods separate duty periods inside a
sequence.

Commuting and co-terminals
Many flight attendants do not live in the city they are based in — the schedule has to account for that.
The commuter
A flight attendant who lives outside their base city and
flies to base — usually standby on a company flight —
before a sequence starts. Commuting happens on
personal time, so report time still governs legality, and
contracts typically add commuter protections when a
connection fails.
Co-terminals and satellites
Two or more airports in the same metro area treated as a
single base for reporting and pay. A sequence can open at
one and close at the other, and a deadhead between them is
often handled as ground transport rather than a flight
segment.

Anatomy of a sequence
Illustrative three-day sequence out of DFW — note it returns to the base it started from.
Duty period 1
DFW → ORD → LGA
3 segments · overnight LGA
Duty period 2
LGA → MIA → CLT
2 segments · overnight CLT
Duty period 3
CLT → PHX → DFW
2 segments · released at base
Closed loop rule
A sequence must begin and end at the same crew base. If a flight attendant finishes away from base, the system
owes them a deadhead or repositioning segment back home.

-- 14 of 19 --

Two sequences sharing segments
Two sequences can share the same flight segments in the middle and still close at different bases.
SHARED SEGMENTS
Sequence A
Base MIA MIA → DFW DFW → PHX PHX → LAX LAX → MIA
Sequence B
Base DFW DFW → PHX PHX → LAX LAX → SFO SFO → DFW
Same flights, two crews
Both sequences remain closed loops: A departs and returns to MIA, B departs and returns to DFW — they simply overlap on
DFW → PHX and PHX → LAX before diverging on the final legs.

-- 16 of 19 --

Terms you will meet in the data
Report time
When the FA must be at the gate, before the first departure
of a duty period.
Block time
Gate-out to gate-in for a segment. The basis for most pay
calculations.
Duty time
Report to release. Capped by regulation and contract.
Rest
Protected time between duty periods. Cannot be shortened
by an assignment.
Layover
The overnight stay away from base between duty periods.
Deadhead
Riding as a passenger to reposition, on duty but not
working.
Turn
A sequence with a single duty period returning to base
same day.
Legality
Whether an assignment satisfies every duty, rest and
qualification rule.
